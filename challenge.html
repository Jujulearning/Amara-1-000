The challenge

Nutrition guidance should reflect the lives mothers actually live.

Maternal nutrition is shaped by more than nutrient requirements. Culture, food access, family traditions, and the healthcare system all influence what mothers eat and the guidance they receive.

Yet nutrition tools do not always reflect the diversity of foods and lived experiences of the communities they aim to serve.

Amara is being built to help close that gap.

Cultural food is not a workaround. It is part of the solution.

The science

1,000 days.

The first 1,000 days shape a lifetime.

From pregnancy to a child’s second birthday, bodies and brains grow faster than at almost any other time in life. Nutrition during this window, for mother and child, is one of the strongest foundations a family can build on.

  1. 01

    Maternal nutrition

    What a mother eats before and during pregnancy supports her own health and her baby’s growth. WHO antenatal care recommendations include nutrition interventions as a core part of routine care.

  2. 02

    Infant growth and development

    The brain and body develop rapidly from pregnancy through age two. Nutrients such as protein, iron, zinc, iodine, choline, folate, and long-chain fatty acids are especially important for early brain development.

  3. 03

    Culturally responsive care

    U.S. dietary guidance encourages people to choose nutrient-dense foods that reflect personal preferences, cultural traditions, and budgets, and for the first time includes guidance for pregnancy, lactation, and birth to 24 months.

  4. 04

    The potential role of digital health

    WHO guidance on digital interventions finds they can strengthen health systems in specific ways, while stressing that they are not a substitute for functioning health services. Amara is designed to complement care, not replace it.
