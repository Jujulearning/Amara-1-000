The challenge

Nutrition guidance should reflect the lives mothers actually live.

Maternal nutrition is shaped by more than nutrient requirements. Culture, food access, family traditions, and the healthcare system all influence what mothers eat and the guidance they receive.

Yet nutrition tools do not always reflect the diversity of foods and lived experiences of the communities they aim to serve.

Amara is being built to help close that gap.

What Flora is not. Flora is a concept in development, not a working product or a validated clinical tool. It is not designed to diagnose, predict, or monitor medical conditions, and it will never replace a doctor, midwife, or dietitian.

The science

1,000 days.

The first 1,000 days shape a lifetime.

From pregnancy to a child’s second birthday, bodies and brains grow faster than at almost any other time in life. Nutrition during this window, for mother and child, is one of the strongest foundations a family can build on.

PregnancyAbout nine months
Year oneBreast milk or formula, then first foods
Year twoFamily foods at the family table
  1. 01

    Maternal nutrition

    What a mother eats before and during pregnancy supports her own health and her baby’s growth. WHO antenatal care recommendations include nutrition interventions as a core part of routine care. 1

  2. 02

    Infant growth and development

    The brain and body develop rapidly from pregnancy through age two. Nutrients such as protein, iron, zinc, iodine, choline, folate, and long-chain fatty acids are especially important for early brain development. 2,3

  3. 03

    Culturally responsive care

    U.S. dietary guidance encourages people to choose nutrient-dense foods that reflect personal preferences, cultural traditions, and budgets, and for the first time includes guidance for pregnancy, lactation, and birth to 24 months. 4

  4. 04

    The potential role of digital health

    WHO guidance on digital interventions finds they can strengthen health systems in specific ways, while stressing that they are not a substitute for functioning health services. Amara is designed to complement care, not replace it. 5

Nutrition works alongside many other influences, including genetics, environment, access to care, and the support around a family. It is powerful, but it is never the whole story. Amara has not yet measured health outcomes; we will share evidence from our own work only as it is gathered.

Our story

The story that
started everything.

Juanita’s sister was pregnant. She received nutrition advice from three places at the same time.

  • Her nutritionist.
  • Her community.
  • Her mother.

All three gave her different answers. None of them agreed. And no one reconciled them.

She was overwhelmed, not because she did not care, but because the system gave her no way to navigate it. The guidance she received did not reflect her food, her culture, or her kitchen.

That confusion is not rare. It is an everyday reality for many mothers. And Amara was built to change it.

“

No one told her which advice was evidence-based, which was tradition, and which was somewhere in between. She was left to figure it out alone.

The founders

Built from two kitchens.
Two cultures. One conviction.

Amara was not built from a research paper. It was built from lived experience and from a belief that a mother’s nutritional plan should include the food she actually cooks.

East African food traditions

Juanita Julius Ndyamukama

Co-founder · MSPH, Johns Hopkins University · Health Policy

Juanita grew up in Tanzania eating mchicha, ugali, and maharage. Foods that have sustained East African mothers for generations. Her sister’s experience with conflicting nutrition advice during pregnancy is what started Amara. Her nutrition and policy expertise shaped every food in Amara’s database. What the system called other, she knew as nourishing.

Black American Southern food traditions

Erica Stephens, MPH

Co-founder · MPH, Johns Hopkins University · Maternal health fellow, Ghana

Erica grew up eating Black Southern food. Collard greens slow-cooked with smoked turkey, black-eyed peas, sweet potato, butter beans. Her grandmother’s kitchen was nutritious. No prenatal handout ever said so. She built Amara’s technical foundation with one conviction: the tools we build should work for the communities most often left out of their design.
