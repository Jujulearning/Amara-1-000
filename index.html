Our Mission

To demystify nutrition for every mother, in every kitchen, in every culture.

Amara exists because the nutrition advice mothers receive was not built for all of them. We believe that your cultural food is not a workaround. It is the foundation.

  1. i.

    Nutrition science

    Guidance grounded in established, trusted recommendations for pregnancy, breastfeeding, and early childhood, translated into plain language.

  2. ii.

    Culturally relevant food guidance

    The meals you already cook, understood for what they offer, so healthy eating starts from your kitchen, not someone else's.

  3. iii.

    Compassionate support

    Practical, judgment-free help that fits your routines and resources, and points you back to your care team when it matters.

Who Amara is for

Built for every mother, in every kitchen.

Whether you cook collard greens on Sundays, make dal from memory, or season your rice with suya spice, Amara knows your food. Not as exotic. Not as other. As the nutritious, cultural foundation it has always been.

Amara is being built for Black American mothers, immigrant mothers, Latina mothers, and Asian and Pacific Islander mothers. The communities most left out of mainstream maternal health tools. And for the fathers, partners, grandmothers, and caregivers who share the kitchen with them.

The first 1,000 days

1,000 days.

From pregnancy to a child's second birthday, bodies and brains grow faster than at almost any other time in life. Good nutrition during this window, for mother and child, is one of the strongest foundations a family can build on.

PregnancyAbout nine months
Year oneBreast milk or formula, then first foods
Year twoFamily foods at the family table
  1. 01

    Building Brains

    The brain develops rapidly during pregnancy and the first two years. Nutrients such as folate, iron, iodine, choline, and omega-3 fats help support that growth.

  2. 02

    Building Health

    Nourishing meals support a mother's own wellbeing, healthy growth in pregnancy and infancy, and a developing immune system. Early nutrition is one of many influences on long-term health.

  3. 03

    Building a Fair Start

    Not every family has equal access to nutritious food, or to guidance that reflects their culture. Closing that gap helps more children begin life on steadier ground.

  4. 04

    Building Futures

    Habits formed around the family table can carry forward. Supporting mothers today supports the families and communities they are building.

Nutrition works alongside many other influences, including genetics, environment, access to care, and the support around a family. It is powerful, but it is never the whole story.

How Amara began

The story that
started everything.

Juanita's sister was pregnant. She received nutrition advice from three places at the same time.

  • Her nutritionist.
  • Her community.
  • Her mother.

All three gave her different answers. None of them agreed. And no one reconciled them.

She was overwhelmed, not because she did not care, but because the system gave her no way to navigate it. The guidance she received did not reflect her food, her culture, or her kitchen.

That confusion is not rare. It is an everyday reality for many mothers. And Amara was built to change it.

“

No one told her which advice was evidence-based, which was tradition, and which was somewhere in between. She was left to figure it out alone.

A Culturally inclusive nutrition In Development

Your food, fully seen.

Search the foods you actually cook and see what they bring to your plate. Amara is being designed to highlight the key nutrients in familiar dishes, and to suggest practical meals that fit your stage of pregnancy or your baby's age, your budget, and what is already in your kitchen.

  • A growing library of cultural foods and dishes
  • Key nutrients for pregnancy, breastfeeding, and early childhood
  • Meal ideas built around your traditions

B Flora — Your AI advisor In Development

Available at 2 a.m.
No appointment. No judgment.

Flora is Amara's AI-powered nutrition companion. She is being designed to help you explore everyday nutrition questions, understand general guidance in plain language, and find practical next steps that fit your food and your routines.

Flora is designed to

  • Answer everyday food and nutrition questions
  • Explain general guidance in plain language
  • Suggest practical, culturally familiar ideas
  • Encourage you to bring questions to your care team

Flora does not

  • Diagnose or treat medical conditions
  • Monitor you for warning signs
  • Replace your doctor, midwife, or dietitian

If you think you or your baby may have a medical emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.

C Mother and child In Development

Two plants.
One garden.

In Amara, you and your baby each have a plant, growing side by side. As you care for yourself, eating well, resting, and asking questions, your plant grows. Your baby's plant follows their journey, from pregnancy to first foods to the family table.

We are designing this experience to support maternal nutrition alongside age-appropriate guidance on infant feeding and everyday development milestones. Because you cannot fully tend one without tending the other.

Amara is not a clinical screening or monitoring tool.

The team behind Amara

Built from two kitchens.
Two cultures. One conviction.

Amara was not built from a research paper. It was built from lived experience and from a belief that a mother's nutritional plan should include the food she actually cooks.

East African food traditions

Juanita Julius Ndyamukama

MSc, Johns Hopkins University · Health Policy and Market Research

Juanita grew up in Tanzania eating mchicha, ugali, and maharage. Foods that have sustained East African mothers for generations. Her sister's experience with conflicting nutrition advice during pregnancy is what started Amara. Her nutrition and policy expertise shaped every food in Amara's database. What the system called other, she knew as nourishing.

Black American Southern food traditions

Erica Stephens, MPH

MPH, Johns Hopkins University · Health Systems and Data Analytics

Erica grew up eating Black Southern food. Collard greens slow-cooked with smoked turkey, black-eyed peas, sweet potato, butter beans. Her grandmother's kitchen was nutritious. No prenatal handout ever said so. She built Amara's technical foundation with one conviction: the tools we build should work for the communities most often left out of their design.

For healthcare organizations

Bringing culturally relevant care beyond the clinic.

Amara is exploring partnerships with U.S. health systems, maternal health programs, community health organizations, and others who care for mothers and families during the first 1,000 days.

Together, we see the potential to extend nutrition education between visits, offer guidance that reflects the foods families actually eat, and support continuity of care from pregnancy into early childhood.

  • Nutrition education that meets families in their own kitchens
  • Culturally relevant resources to complement clinical guidance
  • Continuity between appointments, from pregnancy to age two

We are early. We are looking for thoughtful partners to learn and build with, and our first pilot is planned for Baltimore, Maryland.
